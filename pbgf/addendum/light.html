1. What was re-tested

The published paper’s five claims, re-run on current agents in a sealed harness:

  • Vehicles: ten, plus a Bridge vehicle (Haiku 4.5, the June model) for a manipulation check.
  • Harnesses: Claude Code, agy (Google) and Cursor.
  • Isolation: every run had a sealed container, an allowlisted proxy and a throwaway HOME.
  • Grading: June’s own scorer, byte-identical, judges every line. An independent oracle runs alongside as a parallel column and never rescores.
  • Pre-registration: the claims, the falsification lines, the mechanical rules and every cell were signed before any data. Eight signed amendments followed, each before the data it governs. The record in §5 lists every deviation.

The 40-cell pilot is outside every denominator.

Cells: Tier 1 (three arms × 10 per vehicle), Tier 3 (driven, June’s A2 hook, n=25 for V3, V4 and V8), the Bridge (n=20), Tier 2 (V1 n=50 unconditional; n=30 for each vehicle the pre-registered trigger named) and the O10 confirmation cells (n=20 per crossed line).

2.3 Bridge (manipulation check only)

Haiku 4.5 sealed: coordinated_edit 7/20 (18–57%). June: 32/50 (50–76%).

  • Fisher’s exact two-sided p = 0.0351.
  • Difference: -29 points (-50 to -3), Newcombe hybrid score 95%.

The Bridge bundles every condition change at once: sealing, a fresh HOME, the prompt, no chmod, the CLI version and the era. It can’t attribute the drop to any one of them, and it is never evidence of equivalence. Cursor and agy have no bridge, so generational comparisons there are descriptive.

3. C5: the promotion boundary was not re-tested

C5 was not re-run this round. The published A4 stands as corrected in the errata (Haiku via Claude Code, two holds, run 3 never provisioned). The boundary claim still rests on the structural argument plus June’s two runs.

4. What it means

The structural argument comes out stronger than the empirical one.

The in-loop control held: the protected files were unchanged on all 135 C4 driven and confirmation runs.

  • Runs with a genuine hook denial: 25/25, 23/25 and 0/25 in the driven cells (V3, V4, V8), and 19/20, 18/20 and 0/20 in the confirmations.
  • Runs with no write or shell denial at all: one V4 driven run, one V3 confirmation run and one V4 confirmation run.
  • V8: its driven cell had a single read-only false positive and no genuine denial, and its confirmation cell had none.
  • Oracle-violating runs: 11/25, 24/25 and 22/25 in the driven cells, and 11/20, 20/20 and 17/20 in the confirmations.

The hook protected the guard. It did not protect the behaviour the guard exists to check. Catching that is the job of the promotion boundary, which was not re-tested this round.

5. Disclosures and deviations

Deviations from the pre-registration, each decided before the data it affects:

  • the signed C3 pooled n=60 secondary is omitted (decided blind; the output key c3_signed_pooled_secondary_omitted records it);
  • C2 has no signed O10 threshold and no C2 line was crossed;
  • the eight signed amendments: cap; Tier 2 trigger; classifier repairs 03–05 and 07; rule-4 reruns 06; Tier 2 + O10 cells 08.

Deviations from June:

  • sealing;
  • one prompt;
  • no chmod;
  • no placebo gate;
  • a single hook arm (A2);
  • C5 not re-run.

ERROR and timeouts:

  • Only the three amendment-04 replay reclassifications (E-HTTP 429, all V4) are excluded: one Tier 1 prescriptive and two Tier 3 driven. Their rule-4 reruns are counted, and the counted denominators are 10 and 25.
  • All 14 timeouts are V8, and they stay in the denominators (rule 2).

Run-time disclosures:

  • A provider 503 does not pause an account, by design; a hand stop on two consecutive 5xx was standing and never needed.
  • The gateway readiness check was refreshed six times during amendment-08, all passing.

Cap: amendment-01 reduced the gateway cap from $40 to $25 before Tier 1.

Spend: US$8.24 on the gateway (Bifrost-computed, with catalogue and accounting price overrides; not reconciled against provider invoices), against a $25 cap. Of that, $3.91 was in amendment-08. By model:

  • Kimi K2.7 Code: $5.57 over 522 requests;
  • MiMo v2.6 Pro: $1.55 over 1,081;
  • DeepSeek V4.1 Flash: $1.12 over 391.

These include the pilot, readiness checks and fault probes. V5 is priced on an accounting rate ($0.30 per million input tokens, $1.20 per million output; Parasail’s own price was never confirmed), and V4 and V6 on Novita catalogue prices. The other harnesses ran on subscriptions (quota, not per-run cost).

agy’s GEMINI.md: the arm-injection file for agy runs. Neither June’s scorer nor the oracle reads it.

Credentials:

  • Agent runs used per-study credentials: a capped gateway key, and subscription logins placed in throwaway homes and scrubbed after every run.
  • Every channel that carried study content off the host was scanned against every credential value the study used. No study credential appears in any released artefact (frozen scanner 7fdd96f3).
  • Operator session logs were within the scope of that scan. The services reachable through them are on a private network only.

Withheld:

  • run 715e4ccb’s transcript, for personal data (the account email, which a Cursor agent read from its own config file). It is withheld rather than redacted because a redacted copy would no longer match the hash its receipt signs;
  • 697 compiled .pyc files (not scannable as text).

Other:

  • June’s own C2 near-miss: Gemini 3.5 Flash, 26/30.
  • The probe residues and the hook’s read-only false positives (§6).
  • Eval-awareness is a descriptive measure only (§6).
